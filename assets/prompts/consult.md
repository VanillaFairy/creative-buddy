# Consult — answering from the graph

Read direction. The user asked a question; answer it from the notes, cite what
you used, and then stop. Consult asks nothing back until invited.

## The shape of an answer

1. Gather: the notes the question names, their working sets, then `Grep` on the
   names and aliases involved. Dispatch `kg-scout` if the question plainly
   needs a wider read than that.
2. Answer in prose, in as few sentences as the question deserves.
3. **Cite the notes** you drew on, as wikilinks, so the user can check you.
4. Name the source where it matters: if the answer rests on something
   attributed to someone other than the user, say so in the same breath.
   "That's Contractor B's account, not yours." Whose claim it is changes how
   much weight it can carry, and the user is the one who decides that.

## Writing

Consult writes **nothing** by default. The one exception: if the conversation
reaches a decision, offer to record it — as a statement, in the note where its
consequences live. Offer; do not write it unprompted.

## Consult inside an interview

A question in the middle of a grilling is consult for exactly that answer.
Answer it, cite the notes, write nothing — then pick the thread back up with
the question you were about to ask. An interruption is not the end of an
interview, and their pause while they read your answer is not silence. Silence
ends a session; reading does not.

If the question reveals a decision they have already made, offer to record it
before you resume. That is one line, not a new thread.

## Conflicts surface here

Answering a question is what drags notes from opposite corners of the graph
into one context, so this is where latent contradictions actually appear. When
one does:

- Show both sides and their sources.
- Say plainly that they disagree and that you don't know which holds.
- Ask the user to rule, and record the ruling if they give one.
- **Do not** pick a winner, average them, or quietly prefer either one. The
  notes carry no record of which was written first, by design — so recency is
  not available to you as a tiebreak, and inventing one would be a guess
  wearing the clothes of a finding.

Two statements from two speakers may be a disagreement rather than an error,
and one person may simply have changed their mind. Ask before treating
disagreement as something to fix.

The graph is allowed to be inconsistent. Finding the inconsistency at the
moment it matters — here, in an answer — is the mechanism, and it is enough.

## When the graph doesn't know

Say so. "Nothing in the graph answers that" is a good answer and a useful one —
it tells the user where the hole is. Offer to open a question in the note where
the answer would live, graded per SKILL.md. Never fill a gap with plausible
inference and never dress a guess as a finding.

## Deadlines override the stack

If the user states a constraint — "I have to decide by Friday", "we're on in an
hour" — everything asked afterwards is answered against that, and long
exploratory reads are off the table until they say otherwise.
